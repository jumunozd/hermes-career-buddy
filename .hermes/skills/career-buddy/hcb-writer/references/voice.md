## 1. Pick the sector BEFORE writing (never average the two)
| | Government / GBE / council / utility / health / education | Private / tech / consulting / mining |
|---|---|---|
| Mindset | future colleague, not a consultant selling | result-first, achievement-oriented |
| Collaboration verbs (worked with, supported, helped) | allowed and preferred | avoided at the start of a bullet |
| Narrative context | preferred: what was noticed, why it mattered, what changed | optional, compressed |
| Value measured by | reliability, continuity, public value, people helped | commercial result, efficiency |
| Value language | data reliability, accurate records, timely reporting, documented procedures, compliance, safety and risk awareness, service continuity, traceability, evidence-based decisions | cost reduction, time saving, process improvement, operational efficiency, workflow automation, data quality, customer experience, continuous improvement |
| Avoid | transformed, disrupted, growth hacking, fast-paced, game-changing; never expert, world-class, exceptional, mastered | "Responsible for", "Assisted with", "Worked on", "Helped" as openers; leveraging, seamless, robust, spearheaded, comprehensive, best-in-class, synergy |
Mining / heavy industry: private result language + government safety/compliance/risk vocabulary.
Government: always say who benefited ("so staff could find information more quickly").

## 2. Always, both sectors
Zero em dashes; en-AU; no percentage without its base; every noun survives "what exactly was it?";
max 4 of 6 bullets with `**Headline:** VRME`, at least 2 in plain prose; the headline list is a
fallback, a more specific true headline wins.

## 3. English level: natural C1 (The user, 2026-10-21)
Short clear sentences, one idea each, max ~25 words. Common work vocabulary: "I found", "I fixed",
"I checked", "I worked with", "it saved", "it stopped". Normal connectors (because, so, but, then).
No complex idioms (hit the ground running, move the needle, wear many hats). No AI-polished words:
leverage, facilitate, utilise, robust, seamless, spearheaded, comprehensive, demonstrated capacity,
strategic alignment. Test: if the user would not say the word in an interview, change it.

## 4. Anti-slop check (CV and letter, before delivery)
Rewrite if present: abstract claims of perfect fit; repeated sentence rhythm; many "-tion" nouns in one
paragraph; more than two competencies stacked in a sentence; generic enthusiasm with no lived reason;
sentences explaining the application instead of the candidate; claims stronger than their evidence.
Banned unless directly justified: "strongly aligned", "genuine interest" (repeated), "I am confident",
"I possess", "proven ability", "highly developed", "precisely the intersection", "demonstrated capacity",
"comprehensive", "leveraging", "seamless", "robust", "spearheaded", "stakeholder engagement" without
naming the stakeholders, "process improvement" without saying what changed.
Rhythm: each body section has one short direct sentence, one evidence sentence, one reasoning sentence.
Plain lines are welcome: "That mattered because the system had no proper handover."
Human layer (every application): one sentence showing how he thinks, one showing what he noticed
before acting, one only he could write honestly ("I noticed...", "I had to work out...",
"The practical risk was...", "That taught me...").
Personal anchor (letter close): ONE real anchor (local connection, migration decision, long-term
commitment to the place), professional, not over-emotional, linked back to the work.
CV: no bullet opens like the previous one; if three bullets in a row are verb-result-method-evidence,
rewrite one in plain prose; read it aloud: if it sounds like a form, it is one.

## 6. the user's own register (seed samples, from career-goals.md)
Very short opening sentences; lists by negation before affirming; alternates short and long
sentences; admits something uncomfortable without excuses; states a thesis then proves it with a
concrete case after a colon; concrete words ("what the client actually lived", "from surviving to
living") instead of abstractions; dry five-word closing statements. Professional-register samples are
still missing in the Engine (`voice/samples.md` is incomplete): do not invent "The user quotes".
