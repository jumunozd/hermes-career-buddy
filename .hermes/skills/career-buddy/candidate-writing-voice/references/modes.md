# Context adaptation
The document authoring instructions own format, length and compliance. Apply these voice adjustments within them.

## Professional Applications
CV, cover letter, selection criteria and application responses: precise, relevant and confident. Prioritise the employer’s evidenced need where the format permits, with concrete supported contribution. Keep CV grammar and concise bullets; do not add greetings, first person or calls to action to formats that do not use them. Mandatory criteria and assessment terminology take precedence over stylistic simplification. Formal does not require inflated vocabulary.

## Professional Networking
Recruiters, LinkedIn and former colleagues: warm, clear and easy to answer. A shared memory is appropriate only if real and useful. State job-search intent honestly. “Lend a hand” is approved for a familiar contact, not required for a formal recruiter. Provide verified practical details when useful. Propose a manageable next step without assuming availability.

## Workplace Communication
Colleagues, supervisors and employers: practical and courteous. Give shared purpose for requests. Admit the actual cause of an issue without excessive apology; offer a realistic remedy. Use directness or a softened opening according to relationship and power dynamics, without concealing responsibility.

## Casual Australian English
Relaxed everyday wording and contractions, with correct natural English. Light idioms can fit; do not perform an Australian persona. “Hey mate, too easy! … Give us a shout” was not selected against a plainer alternative; that is not a universal ban on each expression.
