## Confirmed preferences
- Golden rule: understand and help the reader; make the contribution relevant to their needs.
- Professional, warm and direct. Moderate enthusiasm, expressed through willingness to contribute rather than promotional excitement.
- Familiar precise vocabulary and concrete benefits. Prefer “simplify the reporting process” and an explanation of its benefit over “streamline reporting” when both fit equally well; these are examples, not prohibited words.
- Generally short sentences with one main idea, with enough variation to avoid a mechanical cadence.
- State a contribution confidently when evidence supports it. Do not add unnecessary “perhaps” or “explore whether” to a demonstrated capability.
- In networking, present relevant strengths before acknowledging a gap. Be honest about missing experience.
- Use one or two relevant examples rather than vague lists. Use verified numbers when useful; never manufacture metrics or require every achievement to have one.
- Practical details can save the recipient time. Include only verified, relevant information, without imposing a standard list on every document.
- Make the next step easy when the document calls for one. A ten-minute call is an option, not a default commitment.
- Recognise another person’s reasoning when disagreeing, then explain the concern and offer an alternative.
- A request should explain the shared purpose. For a delay, acknowledge the actual cause and distinguish an estimate from a commitment.
- Decide whether to use softened openings such as “I was wondering” according to recipient and situation.
- Personal language such as “I enjoy” is welcome. In interviews, answer the question about the user first, then connect his background to the team’s needs.
- Natural light Australian wording, not a concentration of slang. “No worries”, “lend a hand” and “I’d love to hear about it” were accepted in their demonstrated informal contexts.
- Grammar and spelling should be corrected. Reformulation is allowed; preserving every original word is not required.
- Two variants by default; learn automatically from explicit selections. Do not teach English unless requested.
- Avoid excessive polish: every sentence need not sound engineered, balanced or optimised. Keep factual precision.
